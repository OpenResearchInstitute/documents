### 2. AI/ML and Computing Working Group (FCC Strategic Goals 2, 2.2 & 1.2): ORI is a member

- Co-chairs: Lisa Guess (Ericsson) and Dr. Amit Mukhopadhyay (Verizon).
- FCC liaisons: Martin Doczkat (DFO) and Sean Yun (Alternate DFO), per the October 1 deck.

**Vision** (Hendrickson blog; repeated in the deck):

> "This Working Group will evaluate how artificial intelligence and machine learning can enhance communication systems and network operations. This includes interference mitigation, dynamic resource allocation, advanced sensing, and secure decision automation across both terrestrial and satellite environments. This Working Group's efforts ensure the Commission understands how intelligence‑driven tools—such as AI, quantum computing, robotics, autonomous systems, and IoT—are central to the nation's future competitiveness and can strengthen and safely accelerate the innovation ecosystem."

**Tasks:**

> **a. AI/ML and Networks:** How are neural networks models being applied to traffic pattern prediction and network resiliency, and how might such models potentially bias decision-making as it may affect network traffic management and overall system reliability?

> **b. AI/ML and Security/Reliability:** Describe the landscape of AI-Assisted Zero Trust network architecture implementations to address policy enforcement issues and adversarial device, traffic, and attack detection, and the reliability of automated AI interference detection and mitigation on wireless network management.

> **c. Practicality of AI/ML-based broadband performance and availability analytics to support broadband deployment:** How can AI/ML solutions support competitive market analysis, cybersecurity, network resiliency, emergency preparedness, aspects of the FCC's broadband programs and broadband networks in general?

> **d. AI/ML and Consumer Protection:** What emerging ethical and privacy risks and vulnerabilities should the FCC be aware of related to user data being used for AI model training and optimization on networks increasingly dependent on cloud computing infrastructure?

> **e. Quantum Network, Computing, and Post-Quantum Cryptography:** What is the overlay requirement for quantum network integration into the Internet architecture (including the medium and all HW/SW aspects)? What are essential interoperability standards necessary for quantum protocols? Describe the practicality and scalability of quantum cryptography, and how quantum computing is envisioned to be used on AI-assisted network data and traffic routing management, and the tradeoffs versus other technologies.

> **f. Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** What are the computing, infrastructure, and spectrum needs for robotics and autonomous systems for the FCC to be aware of?

> **g. Convergence of AI and IoT:** How does AI/ML affect IoT architecture, applications, and how do those effects bear on the Internet protocols and security/accountability concerns, including (data velocity, volume, and veracity) challenges in real time network demand and big data computing?

The October 1 deck lists these aligned FCC Strategic Plan 2026–30 goals:

> **STRATEGIC GOAL 2:** Promote National Security And Public Safety
>
> **OBJECTIVE 2.2.** Support Interoperability, Resiliency, Security, And Rapid Restoration Of Critical Communications Infrastructure And Services; And The Public Safety Community's Access To Spectrum Resources
>
> **OBJECTIVE 1.2.** Reduce Barriers To Broadband Deployment

### 1. Emerging Wireless Broadband Working Group (FCC Strategic Goals 1 & 2.3)

- Co-chairs: Manu Gosain (Northeastern University) and Brian Daly (AT&T).
- FCC liaison: Martin Doczkat, per the October 1 deck.

**Vision** (Hendrickson blog):

> "This Working Group will focus on the earliest building blocks of future wireless systems, examining technologies that will underpin 6G and other next‑generation architectures. This includes foundational research related to coverage, latency, reliability, resilience, security, and long‑term U.S. leadership. In alignment with the FCC Strategic Plan and recent areas of interest, this work also supports broader priorities such as 5G/6G evolution, satellites and space, and quantum communications, drones, and sensing, all of which are expected to increase advanced wireless possibilities as technology continues to rapidly evolve."

**Tasks:**

> **a. U.S. role in 6G and IoT standardization:** What specific technical and policy roles in standardization would benefit the U.S. to support its international leadership, promote advancement of next generation technology infrastructures, and incentivize accelerated infrastructure builds across the country?

> **b.** What are the ecosystem, interoperability, standardization, and infrastructure needs for 6G and IoT, and how can the FCC proactively address them?

> **c.** How are 6G and other critical emerging technology standards progressing or expected to progress at standards organizations and international fora?

> **d. Open RAN, AI-RAN, vRAN, etc.:** What are the key points of emerging consensus or misalignment? What solutions and benefits do these capabilities offer or lack with respect to wireless network security accountability (non-repudiation), performance, scalability, and total cost of operations (TCO)?

> **e. Satellite and Non-Terrestrial Networks (NTN):** How can the Commission better support multi-orbital interoperability, supplemental coverage from space (SCS) service quality, backhaul and IoT supportability, cloud integration, and resiliency for new and emerging satellite services?

> **f. Integrated sensing and communications:** What are the applications currently being envisioned for integrated sensing communications (ISAC), and what should the FCC consider to support emerging ISAC services?

> **g. Terrestrial and satellite quantum communications:** How will quantum systems be used for wireless and satellite communications?

### 3. Spectrum Management Reform Working Group (FCC Strategic Goals 1 & 4.2)

- Co-chairs: Dr. Monisha Ghosh (University of Notre Dame) and Dr. Andrew Clegg (Wireless Innovation Forum).
- Sign-up requires naming at least two task items (a–g) and committing to contribute written work on them.

**Vision** (Hendrickson blog):

> "This Working Group will explore new approaches to spectrum efficiency, coexistence, and interference protection as demand grows across federal, commercial, satellite, and emerging technology sectors. This includes advanced sharing frameworks, propagation modeling, and technical tools that support increasingly diverse communications platforms that rely on spectrum. These insights will help the FCC identify opportunities to modernize spectrum‑management systems and maintain a healthy balance between innovation and coexistence."

**Tasks:**

> **a. Data collection and database functionalities requirements for spectrum utilization assessment:** What challenges might the FCC encounter when creating a spectrum utilization assessment of market specific, localized, nationwide, global by services (mobile, satellite, etc.) regarding specific frequency bands?

> **b. Spectrum deployment/demand/interference forecasting:** How might the FCC instill a better long-term strategic planning and phased approach to spectrum repurposing and auctions, in light of the 800 megahertz spectrum goal in the OBBB Act and beyond?

> **c.** How can the interplay between terrestrial and satellite services be complemented in spectrum sharing models to promote more efficient spectrum use?

> **d.** What methodologies for dynamic interference threshold calibration should the FCC consider for coexistence feasibility studies and standardization policy requirements?

> **e. Secure and trustworthy data exchange protocols and interfaces for federal and non-federal operations:** How can the FCC develop trust in data sharing models to facilitate more efficient spectrum sharing while protecting potentially sensitive information about incumbent spectrum use?

> **f.** How might the licensing coordination and system requirements for federal and non-federal bands (techniques involving prioritization, data sharing, international coordination for satellite) be optimized?

> **g. RF propagation modeling methodology and interference analysis:** How might the FCC establish a baseline for frequency coordination, i.e., what methods, references, and technical parameters to apply and rely upon, etc., and other feedback mechanisms for incumbent protection? What factors and methodologies should the FCC be considering as it seeks to repurpose spectrum generally and pursuant to ongoing spectrum studies focused on spectrum bands identified for meeting the 800 megahertz goal required by the OBBB?

*Statutory context for SMR-b and SMR-g: Public Law 119-21, §40002 (139 Stat. 128–130), read from the enrolled law.*

### Where ORI Contributes

These are ORI's mapping, not part of the mandate.

- **Network resiliency** appears in two tasks: AIC-a (AI/ML and Networks) and AIC-c (Broadband analytics).
- **"Networks increasingly dependent on cloud computing infrastructure"** (AIC-d) is the mandate's only reference to the cloud and data-center layer.

## R1. FCC outage reporting scope

- **R1.1 [V]** 47 CFR Part 4, *Disruptions to Communications*. eCFR, current as of September 30, 2026. https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-4
  - **§4.3:** reporting covers cable, wireless (CMRS, paging), wireline, satellite, SS7, IXC/LEC tandem, interconnected VoIP, covered 988 providers, and submarine cable licensees. It excludes "equipment manufacturers or vendors that do not maintain or provide communications networks or services." Cloud, data center, and AI providers are not listed. ORI believes this is a gap in the communications supply chain.
  - **§4.5:** an outage is "significant degradation in the ability of an end user to establish and maintain a channel of communications as a result of failure or degradation in the performance of a communications provider's network."
  - **§4.9:** the reporting threshold is 900,000 user minutes and an outage of at least 30 minutes.
- **R1.2 [V, secondary]** FCC 24-5, Report and Order mandating DIRS reporting (PS Docket 21-346), January 26, 2024. Summary by TLP: https://www.tlp.law/2024/02/01/fcc-releases-adopted-ro-fnprm-mandating-dirs-reporting/. It requires cable, wireless, wireline, and interconnected VoIP providers to file daily DIRS reports once DIRS is activated.
- **R1.3 [V]** FCC 26-34, Third Report and Order (Resilient Networks), adopted May 20, 2026. https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-26-34A1.txt
  - It consolidates the DIRS forms.
  - It adds public-safety broadband networks to DIRS starting November 30, 2026.
  - It has no substantive discussion of cloud, data centers, or AI in it.

## R2. AI traffic and uplink

- **R2.1 [V]** Ericsson Mobility Report, "GenAI data traffic today," June 24, 2025. https://www.ericsson.com/en/reports-and-papers/mobility-report/articles/genai-data-traffic-today-june-2025
  - Generative AI is "0.06 percent of the total network data traffic."
  - Its split is "74 percent downlink and 26 percent uplink," compared with a typical "90-to-10."
  - ChatGPT is 71/29. DeepSeek and Copilot are about 50/50.
  - **Scope (critical):** 21 named standalone AI apps, including ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot, Galaxy AI, Perplexity, and Midjourney. The data is "one week of traffic data collected from a service provider's network in April 2025," extrapolated to monthly usage. "Regular mobile apps that have AI features integrated are not included." "Only traffic that can be clearly identified is covered." The classification method isn't disclosed.
  - The 0.06% is a share of total traffic in both directions. The 74/26 split describes the AI traffic itself.
- **R2.2 [V]** Ericsson blog, "GenAI and mobile traffic," A. Kaur and R. Dehghani, July 31, 2026. https://www.ericsson.com/en/blog/2026/7/genai-and-mobile-traffic
  - Uplink is "just 10 percent of mobile traffic" today.
  - Uplink "could run three times higher by 2031... up to five times higher in high-growth markets."
  - AI glasses use "up to 1.4 Mbps per session."
- **R2.3 [V]** Ericsson Mobility Report, June 2026, landing page. https://www.ericsson.com/en/reports-and-papers/mobility-report/reports/june-2025 (the URL slug says 2025, but the content is 2026). The page lists articles on "AI's effect on uplink" but gives no numbers.
- **R2.4 [V]** Nokia Bell Labs, *Global Network Traffic Report*, 2025. https://www.nokia.com/asset/i/213660
  - AI traffic reaches "921 EB/month by 2034, growing at a CAGR of 23%."
  - Agentic AI traffic grows from "66 EB/month in 2025 to 537 EB/month in 2034."
  - AI's uplink share is "up to 40%," but the "overall network impact will stay modest."
- **R2.5 [V]** Nokia blog, H. Holma, "The AI revolution: Preparing for a surge in 5G uplink traffic," December 17, 2024. https://www.nokia.com/blog/the-ai-revolution-preparing-for-a-surge-in-5g-uplink-traffic/

  - Typical 5G uplink is "10-15Mbps," and often "below 5Mbps."
- **R2.6 [V]** GSMA Intelligence blog, "Beyond Download Speeds…," July 20, 2026. https://www.gsmaintelligence.com/blogs/beyond-download-speeds-preparing-mobile-networks-for-an-ai-driven-uplink-heavy-future

  - It projects that ratio to "narrow to between 1:3 and 1:6 by 2030."

  - Sustained upstream activity is "a novel stress factor for mobile networks."
- **R2.8 [V]** 3GPP SA1 draft S1-261246 (TR 22.870, 6G), Table 14.1.8-6, "AI traffic characteristics," February 2026. https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/Meetings_3GPP_SYNC/SA1/Inbox/drafts/draft_S1-261246_S1-261201%20was%20S1-261200%20Table%2014.1.8-6%20AI%20traffic%20characteristics-%20after2call.docx. ## R10. Working group presentations (committee records, we summarize and attribute, we don't repost slides without the presenter's OK)

- **R10.1** Javier Antich (Cisco), presentation to the FCC TAC AI/ML and Computing Working Group, October 14, 2026. *Notes taken live by Michelle. Slides were filed to the working group's OneDrive and carry Cisco's default "partner confidential" footer.*
  - **Definition of AI traffic:** inference requests to LLM **API endpoints** only, such as api.openai.com, api.anthropic.com, openrouter.ai, api.mistral.ai, and the Hugging Face inference API. Consumer chat sites like claude.ai and chatgpt.com are **excluded** as "just websites": that traffic goes from the browser to the server, and the study tracks the API servers. Only inference requests were analyzed, not other behavior.
  - **Vantage point:** a monitoring engine at the service provider's access edge. Mobile, fixed wireless access (FWA), and fixed users are combined, with **no breakdown by access type**. A breakdown is a goal for the next report.
  - **Data period:** a little more than a year, mostly 2025 into early 2026. Collected weekly.
  - **Main finding:** AI and LLM flows are **longer, with a lower average rate**, than non-AI flows. The analysis uses total volume and average rate, not peak rates.
  - **Mapping to our traffic classes (ORI's reading):** this measures programmatic and agent traffic, classes (d) and (e), and possibly part of (b). It doesn't measure consumer app or web chat, class (a), which is what Ericsson R2.1 measured. **The two studies measure almost completely different traffic, so they complement each other and can't be compared directly.**
