I have some news about the "800 MHz spectrum pipeline", from the OBBB
Act, and our microwave bands.

This is the short version from reading the statute (P.L. 119-21, Sec.
40002), and from paying careful attention at yesterday's kick-off
meeting of the FCC Technological Advisory Council meeting in
Washington, D.C.

Open Research Institute (501(c)(3) all volunteer non-profit) is a TAC
member, and I'm one of the representatives of ORI. These are my own
views, not TAC positions.

NTIA must find 500 MHz of federal spectrum between 1.3 and 10.5 GHz
for full-power commercial mobile broadband, with the first 200 MHz
named by July 2027. Bands are picked to maximize auction proceeds net
of federal relocation costs. 3.1-3.45 and 7.4-8.4 GHz are excluded.
Separately, the FCC must auction 300 MHz, with no frequency range
specified beyond 100 MHz at 3.98-4.2 GHz.

For us?

2.3/2.4, 5.7 and 10 GHz are all *inside the window and eligible where
they share with federal users*. Nothing in the law names amateur
radio, and none of the bands NTIA is studying today (1675-1695 MHz,
2.7-2.9, 4.4-4.94, 7.125-7.4 GHz) are ours. Eligible, not targeted.
Higher bands like 24 GHz are outside NTIA's window.

The best defense is a visible record of use. Contest and rover logs,
beacon monitoring, rain scatter and EME activity, published
propagation data. If a band of ours ever shows up in an NTIA or FCC
proceeding, organized technical comments with that data behind them
will carry far more weight than panic posts.

I'm on this closely. So is Greg Lapin at ARRL. He represents ARRL on
TAC, and is a serious and positive resource for advisories to the FCC.

Both of us could use *all of your help to defend our bands*. Please,
operate. Log your contacts. Publish the logs. If you are doing any
sort of experimental, educational, public service, public safety, or
radio service work? Write about it and publish it.

Thank you!
-Michelle W5NYV
